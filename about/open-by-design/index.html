03 / Open by design

Your cubacadabra game should not be trapped inside cubacadabra.

Creator-first should describe the architecture, not just the marketing.

Openness is not just a philosophy here. It can be part of the competitive strategy. If creators can inspect the engine, understand networking, use documented file formats and scripting APIs, and submit fixes, the relationship changes from “trust our black box” to “help shape the tools you depend on.”

If the runtime and formats are open, a creator also has more confidence that their work will remain usable if the company ever changes direction. That is a real answer to platform risk: the work does not simply disappear behind a proprietary executable.

Open where openness helps

  • Rust engine and common runtime
  • Web, iOS, and Android clients
  • SDKs, Luau bindings, and creator tools
  • World and asset formats
  • Protocol definitions and local servers
  • Sample games and public documentation

Private where secrecy protects

  • Production safety models and abuse heuristics
  • Anti-cheat detection and ban-evasion signals
  • Fraud rules, payment risk, and private operations
  • Moderator and law-enforcement tooling
  • Credentials, secrets, and infrastructure details

Open anything where openness benefits creators, interoperability, trust, or contribution. Keep private anything where secrecy materially raises the cost of abuse.
